## 3. Challenges and patterns

### 3.1 Review-layer latency infrastructure failure — 3 stories

The three adversarial review layers failed to return before their reviews closed on Stories 32.3, 32.0, and 32.1 — then delivered ~2h *post-commit* (32.3: 15 deduped items/6 MEDIUM; 32.1: 6 items/2 MEDIUM; 32.0: never delivered). Three distinct failure modes: **slow layers** (nested background agents, plausibly quota-starved — the day's API-limit events correlate, and post-upgrade reviews saw all layers return on time), **silent death** (32.0: idle notifications, no findings, indistinguishable from "found nothing" without a delivery receipt), and **stale-tree reviews** (32.1's edge layer generated its own diff *after* the review's patches landed and reported an already-fixed item).

Cost: one full extra story (32.4), two lead triage passes, post-commit churn. Benefit confirmed: reviews with live layers (32.2, 32.4) were strictly better — the epic's only HIGH and the IPv6 MEDIUM came from layers, not reviewer-direct.

**Disposition: RECORD-AND-OBSERVE (Project Lead decision).** No process change now — the plan upgrade may have removed the starvation cause (both post-upgrade reviews had full layer participation). **Recurrence trigger:** any Epic 33 review closing on silent layers ⇒ revisit bounded-close (review may not close while layers outstanding), frozen-diff-for-layers (late returns can't be stale), and delivery receipts (no findings file = not done, never "found nothing").

### 3.2 Hand-count tallies drifted three times despite Rule #51

The lead's own AC said "22 items" where 23 were enumerated (caught by dev + auditor); the 32.3 test counts needed correction; the auditor flagged "story record not reconciled post-QA" on consecutive stories. Rule #51 exists; prose numbers kept being authored before being counted.

**Disposition:** noted, no new rule — Rule #51 already covers it; the lead applies it at *story-creation* time too, not only at close.

### 3.3 Lead smokes caught zero product defects — and 7 smoke-script bugs

Across 4 lead smokes, every red iteration was a script bug (guessed API signatures, fixture contracts, a `"__proto__" in obj` trap), never a product defect. Analysis (Project Lead concurred): primarily the automated tiers genuinely leveled (§2), plus a probe-before-write discipline gap in throwaway scripts (Rules #14/#16 apply to smoke scripts too). Model attribution (Opus → K3 switch) examined and **not indicted**: failures are fully explained by signature-guessing, and the Epic 31 Opus lead had its own tooling-failure class at similar rates (Rule #55 was born under it). **Disposition:** keep-as-habit (probe-first smoke scripts; `defects_caught` tracked in the cycle log — if smokes go 2–3 epics at zero, re-aim them at what the tiers can't see). Not codified.

## 8. Key takeaways

1. **Adversarial diversity is load-bearing, not decorative.** Reviews with live layers caught the epic's only HIGH; reviews without them needed a cleanup story. The net works, but it costs more than the layers do.
2. **The automated tiers caught up to the lead smoke.** Zero smoke-caught defects is what success looks like when process gates do real handshakes with mutation verification — the smoke's job is now independent re-verification, and its own scripts deserve the same probe-first discipline as production code.
3. **Mechanical counting is a posture, not a rule you pass once.** Three tally drifts in one epic, all caught by re-derivation — including one by the lead who wrote the rule's favorite counterexample into an AC.
4. **F2 shipped as designed: multi-agent by construction.** Because the editors write a plain file and the servers remain the sole enforcement authority, every MCP client picks the policy up with a restart — no per-client governance channel exists anywhere.
